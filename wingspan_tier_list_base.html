Wingnut Bird Tiers

How good is each Wingspan bird card? These lists come from an AI that taught itself to play Wingspan by playing millions of games against copies of itself, and was then made to play many thousands more while we recorded which birds helped it win. A bird in S tier reliably helps the player who gets it; one in F tier tends to hold them back. The numbers measure value to this AI: it is strong, but it may misjudge some birds a human would play well. Each tab is a separate list; pick one, then tap a card for details.

When activated When played Once between turns No power Edge color = power type. Big number = the bird's printed points; small number at the bottom = its value in this tab.

The number on each card is how many points of final score margin (your score minus the best opponent's) a player gains, on average, when this bird is drawn into their hand instead of a typical card. +3.0 means drawing it is worth about 3 points; −1.0 means a typical card would have done better.

How it's measured. Whenever a card comes off the top of the deck, which bird it is comes down to luck, so comparing how those players finished is a fair test, like a coin-flip experiment repeated hundreds of thousands of times. Values are averaged over the whole game, from the starting hand to round 4. The AI is our strongest base-game player; it wins about 80% of 3-player games against two copies of a hand-tuned rule-based bot.

The number on each card is how much more often (in percentage points) a player wins when they keep this bird in their starting hand. +20 means keeping it raises the win rate by 20 points, for example from 33% to 53%.

How it's measured. At the start of each game we randomly decided which of the five dealt birds each player kept, like a coin flip for each card, then let the AI play normally. Comparing winners who kept a bird with those who didn't gives a fair measure. It only covers the opening; a bird that is great to draw late, such as a high-point bird for the last round, can look poor here. Same AI as the Base game tab.

How to read these lists

  • Tiers are cut from each tab's number: S is the best, F the worst. The cut-offs are shown under each tier letter.
  • Small differences don't mean much. Each value has some uncertainty (the "± (95%)" column in the table). In the Base game tab it is usually about ±0.15 points, so two birds within a few tenths of each other are effectively tied.
  • These are values to this AI. The AI learned entirely by playing itself, with no human strategy given to it. It is strong, but it has a style of its own. If it rarely uses a certain kind of power, birds with that power will rank lower than a human expert who uses them well might expect.
  • Tap any card to see its power, how often the AI plays it, which round it is usually played in, how many points it typically scores, and how often the AI uses its power when given the chance.

What the AI values (Base game tab)

  • Card draw in the grassland and wetland is king. The top birds are Franklin's Gull (+6.4), Killdeer (+4.6), Spotted Towhee (+4.4), Barn Swallow (+3.7), American Robin (+3.5) and Purple Martin (+3.2). Most of them let you draw cards or tuck cards for value every time you take a common action. The AI builds its game around taking eggs and cards in the same turn.
  • Cheap birds beat expensive ones. Birds that cost no food average +1.6 points, 1-food birds +0.5, 2-food birds +0.1, and 3-food birds −0.4.
  • Low printed points usually means a good bird. Birds printed with 0–2 points average +0.8; birds with 6–9 points average −0.5. The game balances big point values with weak powers and high costs, and the AI prefers the powers.
  • Power type: pink (between-turn) birds average +0.7, brown (when activated) +0.1, and white (when played) −0.5. Birds that can live in two habitats average +0.9; one-habitat birds −0.2.
  • Bottom of the list: Tufted Titmouse, Lazuli Bunting, Song Sparrow, Hermit Thrush, Western Meadowlark and Mourning Dove (each about −1.0). Mostly these are conditional or slow powers on birds that don't otherwise fit the AI's plan.

Surprises worth knowing

  • Mourning Dove and Wood Duck rank low even though many players rate them highly. The AI rarely takes the "gain food" action (about 4 times per game), so forest powers fire less often for it, and Wood Duck's 3-food cost is hard to pay in the base game. Chipping Sparrow, by contrast, sits in A tier.
  • Starting hand vs. drawn later. The two Base game tabs mostly agree (correlation 0.95), but a bird's value depends on when you get it. Engine birds such as Spotted Towhee are worth a lot at the start of the game and almost nothing in round 4. High-point birds are the opposite.
  • The AI often turns down powers. For example, it uses Franklin's Gull and Killdeer (discard an egg to draw 2 cards) only about 63% of the time, and it skips powers that also help opponents, such as Eastern Phoebe (used 62% of the time).
